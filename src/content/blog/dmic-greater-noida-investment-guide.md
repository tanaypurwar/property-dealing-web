**The short answer:** the DMIC township in Greater Noida has real foundations: trunk infrastructure that the government says is complete, factories already running, and a large developer that has paid for land there. Much of what makes it attractive, though, is still being built. It suits buyers who can wait several years, and only for projects that are RERA-registered. As of 20 October 2026, Godrej's project there is not.

## What "DMIC Greater Noida" actually is

DMIC is the Delhi–Mumbai Industrial Corridor, a central programme to build planned industrial cities along the freight railway between the two cities. In Greater Noida, its project is the **Integrated Industrial Township Greater Noida (IITGN)**, run by a company jointly set up by the Centre's corridor agency (NICDC) and the state, called IITGNL.

The basics, from the government's own updates:

- **Size and land use:** about 747.5 acres, a little over half of it for industry, about 6% for commercial and mixed use, and the rest for housing, roads and open space.
- **Stage:** the company was set up in 2014, and the government reports that major trunk infrastructure (roads, water, power, drainage) is complete. The township was dedicated to the nation on 25 January 2024.
- **Who's there:** NICDC lists companies including Haier, with some units operational and others under construction.

This matters because a planned township is not a promise on a brochure. The land is sold by a government company through e-auctions, the layout is fixed in advance, and the trunk services are already in the ground.

## Why buyers are looking at it

### 1. Jobs close by

Housing demand follows employers. Besides the factories inside the township, the government is building two large hubs next to it:

- a **Multi-Modal Logistics Hub at Dadri** (freight: rail, road and warehousing), and
- a **Multi-Modal Transport Hub at Boraki** (passengers: railway, interstate buses and a planned metro link).

When they were approved in December 2020, the government said the two together would cover about 1,208 acres and create jobs for around 1,00,000 people. That job figure is a projection, not a count.

### 2. Freight and road connections that already work

The Dadri end of the Dedicated Freight Corridor is operating: on 19 February 2026, the railway freight company reported a record 56 trains interchanged in a day at New Dadri. A 3 km rail link from New Dadri to the logistics hub has an approved project report. By road, the township sits by NH-91 and close to the Eastern Peripheral Expressway.

### 3. The airport is now open

Noida International Airport at Jewar was scheduled to start commercial flights on 15 June 2026, and reports say it did. It is roughly 40 minutes from the township by road, according to marketing material we've seen; we haven't timed it ourselves.

### 4. A large developer has committed money

On 1 June 2026, Godrej Properties won an e-auction for a **23.2-acre (93,905 sq. m) residential land parcel** inside the township. Godrej says it plans premium group housing there and estimates its revenue potential at over ₹7,000 Cr, based on current assumptions. That figure is the company's projection of its own sales, not a forecast of what your flat will be worth. Still, a listed developer paying for land through an open auction is a meaningful signal of confidence.

## About "Godrej Global Business City"

You may have seen the project advertised online as **Godrej Global Business City**, **Godrej GBC** or **Godrej DMIC**, with prices such as "₹1.35 Cr onwards" and requests for an expression of interest (EOI). Three things to know:

- **Those sites are run by marketing partners, not by Godrej.** At least one says so in its own disclaimer.
- **Godrej hasn't announced a project name or prices** in its own statements that we could find.
- **The project isn't RERA-registered.** As of 20 October 2026, the partner sites themselves say registration is in process. Under UP RERA, a project shouldn't be advertised or booked before it is registered.

We'll list it, with its RERA number and an all-inclusive price sheet, once it's registered.

## Other developers in the township

IITGNL has run schemes for several group housing and commercial plots, not just Godrej's. We haven't found a public list of the other winners yet. As other projects get RERA registration, we'll check and add them. If a broker offers you a project "in DMIC", ask which plot it is and when IITGNL allotted it.

## The risks, plainly

- **Timelines.** The logistics hub, the Boraki transport hub and the planned metro link are still in development. We couldn't find a 2026 construction update for either hub. Infrastructure in NCR has often arrived years after its first announced date.
- **Jobs are projected, not yet there.** The 1,00,000 figure assumes both hubs are finished and occupied.
- **Everyday life is still a drive away.** The hospitals, malls and universities that marketing material lists for the area are 12 to 20 minutes away by its own estimates.
- **Supply.** If several housing plots launch around the same time, resale and rents can stay flat for a while.
- **Paying before registration.** An EOI paid for an unregistered project has fewer protections. The refund terms are whatever the developer or broker writes down.

## Who it suits, and who it doesn't

**It can suit you if** you can hold for several years, you or your tenants are likely to work in the industrial or logistics belt, and you're buying in a RERA-registered project with a construction-linked payment plan.

**It probably doesn't suit you if** you need to move in within two or three years, you're counting on quick resale gains, or the deal depends on paying before registration.

## Checks before you pay anything

1. **RERA registration.** Search the project on [up-rera.in](https://www.up-rera.in). A real number looks like `UPRERAPRJ123456/MM/YYYY`.
2. **Land allotment.** Confirm the developer holds the plot from IITGNL. Ask for the allotment letter or lease deed.
3. **Collection account.** Pay only into the project's RERA collection account named in the registration.
4. **EOI terms in writing.** If you pay an EOI, get the amount, the refund conditions and the refund timeline on letterhead.
5. **All-inclusive price.** Ask for the full breakup: BSP, PLC, IFMS, parking, club, lease rent, GST, stamp duty and registry. "Onwards" prices with an asterisk are not a price.
6. **Payment plan.** Prefer a plan linked to construction stages over one linked only to dates.
